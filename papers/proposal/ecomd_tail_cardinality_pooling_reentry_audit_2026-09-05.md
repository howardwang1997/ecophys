# EcoMD tail-cardinality pooling re-entry audit

**Date:** 2026-09-05 NZST  
**Mode:** D−3 outcome-blind theorem, repository-causal-chain, and primary-work trigger audit  
**Archetype:** `simulator_method`  
**Decision:** **`not_trigger`. Tail-index-only cardinality pooling is closed. It does not authorize a
new discovery cycle, topic card, implementation, simulator run, SSH connection, or GPU job.**

## 1. Why this audit was admissible

The simulated-market family is saturated. This was not a new candidate-harvesting cycle. It tested
one concrete possible remover of the written blocker on
`fluctuation_aware_population_size_transfer` and the failed
`clt_on_excess_demand_mechanism` route:

> Can a learnable, tail-index-aware aggregation or readout preserve heavy-tailed aggregate laws
> when a particle simulator is trained at small population size and evaluated at large size?

The proposed repair would qualify as a trigger only if it supplied a projectively consistent
operator and theorem that existing graph-size extrapolation, learned pooling, mesoscopic closure,
neural renormalization, and stable-limit theory do not already cover. EcoMD could be an application
only after the particle-count intervention had a market-native meaning.

## 2. Frozen rival explanations and discriminating result

The native historical object is the stationary return-tail law as the latent particle count changes.

- **H1 — aggregation/readout smoothing:** an inappropriate sum or mean pooling rule destroys the
  tail as cardinality grows; learning the right tail-aware pooling exponent repairs transfer.
- **H2 — observation-stage mixture:** the raw aggregate flow keeps essentially the same tail shape,
  while nonlinear impact, changing scale, fixed additive price noise, and a finite-sample tail
  estimator create the apparent return-tail change.
- **H3 — dependence change:** marginal tail index is insufficient; changing spatial or temporal
  extremal clusters changes the aggregate stable law, so no scalar tail-index normalization can
  transfer it.

A positive discriminating result would require all of the following:

1. an aggregation operator whose normalization is identified from observable state rather than a
   fitted cardinality label;
2. a theorem for dependent, regularly varying graph messages that uses enough of the tail process
   or cluster law to predict the aggregate path law;
3. invariance under a legal refinement that splits one physical unit into weighted replicas;
4. strict failure of sum, mean, PNA/GNP-style learned pooling, mesoscopic-SDE, and learned-RG parents;
   and
5. preregistered validation on at least two independently generated dynamic systems.

A null result is also useful: it distinguishes an invalid readout story from the repository's
already documented observation-stage ambiguity.

## 3. Repository causal-chain audit

The initial motivation does not match the producing EcoMD system.

1. The exp-123/125/126 production configuration uses `pairwise_kind: stochastic_mlp`, not MACE-lite.
   MACE-lite is a historical failed architecture.
2. `ExcessDemandPrice.step` forms raw demand as
   \(D_N=\kappa\sum_i \Delta s_i\). The production configuration leaves `ed_normalize` at its
   default `false`, then applies the concave impact map and adds price noise.
3. The committed exp-125 result has nearly flat raw-demand Hill estimates over
   \(N=100,\ldots,30000\): SPX 4.61--4.81 and BTC 4.68--4.75. Over the same sweep, the return Hill
   estimate rises from about 5.5 to 8.6--8.8.
4. The repository's later audit already states that this is not a clean CLT mechanism result: the
   nonlinear-impact/fixed-noise mixture must be separated with zero-price-noise and fixed-SNR
   controls. Those controls were not completed as a new causal result.

Therefore H1 is not the supported explanation. Changing MACE readout or replacing a mean pool cannot
explain a result generated by the stochastic-pair model in which the measured pre-impact aggregate
tail is flat.

## 4. Exact reduction: a marginal tail index cannot select the pooling law

Let \(Z\) be symmetric and regularly varying with tail index \(\alpha=3/2\). Consider two arrays
whose node marginals are identical:

\[
\text{independent world: }X_i=Z_i, \qquad
\text{common-shock world: }X_i=Z.
\]

For \(S_N=\sum_{i=1}^N X_i\), the independent world has a non-degenerate stable limit under a
normalization of order \(N^{1/\alpha}=N^{2/3}\), up to a slowly varying factor. In the common-shock
world, \(S_N=NZ\), so its non-degenerate normalization is order \(N\). Both worlds expose the same
nodewise tail index, but require incompatible cardinality scaling. The finite-variance analogue is
the familiar \(\sqrt N\) versus \(N\) contrast.

Hence an operator of the form

\[
\mathcal A_N(X_1,\ldots,X_N)=N^{-c(\widehat\alpha)}\sum_i X_i
\]

cannot be distribution-preserving across even these two dependence structures. Estimating
\(\alpha\) more accurately does not repair the missing cluster law. This is consistent with
[Basrak, Krizmanić and Segers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.1345): for dependent regularly varying
sequences, extreme clustering can change the Lévy triple of the stable limit.

The obvious independent-node repair is not a new ML principle. It is the generalized stable-limit
normalization. The only nontrivial residual would have to learn or otherwise identify a spectral
tail process or extremal-cluster functional under declared graph and temporal dependence.

## 5. Observation maps do not rescue the claim

If \(D\) is regularly varying with survival-tail index \(\zeta\), then the power impact map

\[
g_\delta(D)=\operatorname{sign}(D)c|D|^\delta
\]

has index \(\zeta/\delta\). Multiplication by an \(N\)-dependent positive constant changes scale,
not the asymptotic index. Adding a light-tailed noise term also leaves the asymptotic heavy-tail
index unchanged, although it can strongly move a finite-sample Hill estimate through crossover and
signal-to-noise effects. Thus the old rise in the measured return Hill statistic, alongside flat
raw-demand Hill, is not evidence that a cardinality pool erased the asymptotic tail.

There is a second identification failure. Split one latent agent with state \(x\) and economic
weight \(w\) into \(m\) identical replicas with weights \(w/m\). A weighted economic aggregate can
remain unchanged while the latent count changes from \(N\) to \(N+m-1\). Any unweighted
\(N\)-dependent tail correction changes under this observationally equivalent refinement. EcoMD
has no observed trader unit or weight map that selects one representation, so its latent particle
count is not a market-native treatment.

## 6. Primary-work collision screen

The bounded nearest-neighbour audit finds that every easy component is occupied:

- [Yehudai et al. (ICML 2021)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/yehudai21a.html) prove that graph
  size generalization can fail when local-structure distributions change with size.
- [Ko et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06210) already propose an end-to-end learnable
  \(L^p\)-like pooling family specifically for extrapolation.
- [Corso et al. (NeurIPS 2020)](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/99cad265a1768cc2dd013f0e740300ae-Abstract.html)
  combine multiple statistics with degree scalers that generalize sum aggregation.
- [Freitas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14918) use finite-memory equivariant graph networks
  for correlated tracer-particle dynamics and show that memory is essential for recovering
  intermittent heavy-tailed acceleration statistics.
- The existing route audit already records direct parents for finite-population mesoscopic SDE
  learning and neural small-to-large renormalization.

These papers do not appear to supply the exact tail-process/projective operator described above.
That absence is not a positive result: no such operator, estimand, consistency theorem, or
two-system truth contract has been constructed here. Combining an EVT feature with GNP/PNA or a
memory GNN would be a composition of parents.

## 7. Hard-gate decision

| Gate | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Repository mechanism | **fail** | Producing model is not MACE; raw demand tail is flat while the later return statistic changes. |
| Exact novelty | **fail** for the proposed scalar method | \(N^{-1/\alpha}\) is stable-limit normalization; learned pooling and degree scaling are direct parents. |
| Identifiability | **fail** | Equal marginal tail indices permit incompatible independent and common-shock aggregation laws. |
| Market-native control | **fail** | Latent \(N\) changes under agent splitting without changing the economic aggregate. |
| Irreducible theorem | **absent** | No tail-process-aware, refinement-consistent dynamic transfer theorem has been supplied. |
| Truth and transfer | **absent** | No two independent heavy-tail dynamic systems with a frozen common target are contracted. |

The scalar tail-index-pooling formulation is therefore `failed_closed`. A diagnostic ICLR survival
estimate is at most 2--5%; this is not a prospective full-T0 forecast and is not entered into the
forecast calibration ledger. The fatal reasons are mechanism mismatch, exact reduction,
non-identification, and non-native control—not the probability label.

## 8. Exact re-entry condition

Re-audit this family only when a written object exists that:

1. estimates or represents the relevant spatial-temporal tail process or cluster law rather than
   only a marginal tail index;
2. proves a non-degenerate aggregate path-law transfer result under declared dependence and shows a
   boundary where scalar stable normalization, GNP/PNA, mesoscopic closure, and neural RG fail;
3. is projectively consistent under weighted unit splitting and graph-resolution refinement; and
4. has an outcome-blind two-system benchmark with analytical or gold-standard rare-event truth.

EcoMD may enter only after an observed economic unit and observation map make its population-size
intervention invariant. Until then, `candidate_harvest_authorized: false`.
