## 3. Why an allocation-rule switch is inactive in the stated new mechanism

Under the first paper's stated empty-start, sequential, unit-offer mechanism,
an accepted non-crossing buy offer must strictly exceed the current best bid;
an accepted sell offer must strictly undercut the current best ask. An offer
equal to its own-side best is rejected. Inductively, the stated operations
cannot create two same-side resting offers at the same price: insertion is
strictly better than every currently resting offer on that side, and executions
only remove offers. The standing-best-only interpretation is even more restrictive.

Thus a crossing offer has no equal-price maker tie to resolve. Holding policies,
information and semantic random draws fixed, replacing FIFO with uniform unit
selection at the execution price has no mechanical effect on such paths.
This is a standard treatment-support observation, not a new theorem. It does
not claim the public software was executed or that any implementation is faulty.

Allowing agents to join the current best price would add a new legal action
and change the market. That may define a future experiment, but it is not an
already supplied second implementation of the repository's frozen treatment.
Likewise, changing prompts or announcing a different rule could change LLM
behavior even when allocation is mechanically unchanged; that would introduce
an information treatment, not isolate the proposed queue-allocation effect.

The source's earlier nonconvergence neighbor also uses a different matching
description, as the new paper itself notes in Section2. The discussion does
not provide a matched model disagreement about the repository's priority arm.
