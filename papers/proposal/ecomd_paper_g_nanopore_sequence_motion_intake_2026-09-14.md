One standard control clarifies measurement units. If R reads from one molecule/pore obey Y_r=mu_s+B+epsilon_r, with an unobserved shared random offset B of variance tau^2 and independent noise variance sigma^2, then Var(mean(Y))=tau^2+sigma^2/R. With Gaussian terms and two equal-prior signal means separated by delta, the optimal scalar classification error is Phi[-delta/(2 sqrt(tau^2+sigma^2/R))]. Independent noise averages away; shared uncertainty need not. A separately measured reference can remove that nuisance under additional assumptions. This is an illustrative random-effects identity, not evidence that any cited method suffers this failure, and not a new calibration-transfer topic.

The native assays differ: a motor pulls a charged chain in the older reader, whereas tPAL probes a tethered N terminus as the peptide is chemically shortened. A DNA HMM cannot be assumed to bridge those physical differences. Conversely, the new assay does not make generic classification or overlap-graph decoding novel.
