## What the physical study already establishes

[Gementzopoulos, Sedky and Jones, PR Fluids 9, 014701 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevFluids.9.014701)
compares experimental gust loads and vorticity with Kuessner's model. The
accepted manuscript's Section IV attributes the different lift decline to
LEV detachment and wake-circulation sign, contrasting with delayed bound-
circulation response. It also identifies why the initial lift can agree.
This mechanism comparison is published prior work, not an unresolved discovery
created by this audit. The suggested repair includes modelling leading-edge
separation with numerical vortices.

Section II distinguishes flat-plate/sine-squared and NACA0012-64/trapezoidal
campaigns and explicitly avoids treating them as a direct comparison.
Consequently, changing leading-edge shape is not isolated from gust profile
in that cross-campaign comparison. This does not invalidate within-campaign
findings. Section III's gust-width argument has unspecified constants and
references a prior CFD width variation; it supplies no new universal scaling
claim for Paper G. Selected text was read through the official accepted
manuscript, with the published abstract used for bibliographic confirmation.

## What informative modes mean in the newer ML study

[Koshikawa and Fukami, arXiv:2607.26683v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26683v1),
Sections 2 and 4, studies Re=5000 vortex-gust LES. Its extractor explicitly
takes both a current Q-criterion field and the future target, and uses a
mutual-information decomposition. This is a target-conditioned analysis;
it does not by itself specify a deployable predictor using only present data.
No leakage or implementation-failure claim follows from that distinction.

The conclusion explicitly proposes localized perturbations and subsequent
evolution as a way to validate physical causality. Thus generic intervention
validation of informative structures already appears in this source. The
inspected text describes that validation as future work, rather than reporting
an executed intervention test. The force-element comparison concerns
instantaneous structure contributions; it is not the same response as delayed
lift. Neither this LES nor its target agrees with the earlier physical
campaign closely enough to call them opposed same-contract models.

## Reusable decision boundary

Three questions must stay separate: explaining an observed future outcome,
predicting an unknown future outcome, and changing that outcome through a
specified physical action. Evidence for one can motivate another but does
not establish it. Likewise, an edit of a derived Q field is not yet a specified
velocity/pressure/boundary intervention. A future proposal must supply the
admissible native-state operation and its response, rather than assume that
an arbitrary image-space edit is physically realizable.

This is a scope requirement, not a new impossibility theorem. No claim that
all informative modes lack physical value, that every perturbation is invalid,
or that the original physical mechanism question has no remaining subtleties
is warranted. Separate ensembles can still assess mean responses; this audit
does not impose paired measurements on every possible estimand.
