## 1. Decision first

[Leclere and Rosenbaum (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03115), submitted after the
repository's order-flow and mean-field closures, gives a real new mathematical model. Agents have
heterogeneous forecast horizons, aggregate positions create transient market impact, and a balance
condition can cancel a rough predictable signal from the observed price. After identifying a
dimensionless impact propagator with the one induced by a Hawkes order-flow model, the balance
condition becomes

\[
  \alpha=\lVert h\rVert_1.
\]

This is an interesting compatibility relation, but it does **not** reopen an executable EcoMD or
ICLR topic. The equality is obtained only after two model identifications: the unobserved fraction
of anticipated impact, `alpha`, is not independently measured, and the paper explicitly warns that
its forecast-unit positions and Hawkes unit-trade flow do not share an absolute impact scale.
Fitting both sides from the same anonymous tape would validate the imposed decomposition rather
than distinguish endogenous efficiency from a rival explanation.

The strongest apparent extensions also fail. Learning the forecast-horizon distribution is not
globally identified even with the common signal and aggregate position observed; an exact
within-model counterexample is given below. Observed price regularity identifies at most a threshold
on the near-zero horizon mass once the martingale component caps regularity at one half. Finite-agent
corrections reduce to ordinary empirical-operator fluctuation and mean-field central-limit theory,
while EcoMD's latent particle count still fails the market-native agent-splitting gate. The source
therefore removes no recorded blocker and creates no experiment or compute authorization.

## 2. Frozen question contract

### Market-native object

The proposed object is the joint law of a common forecasting feature, aggregate positions or signed
flow, the impact response, and the observed price across forecast horizons.

### Rival explanations

- **H1 -- heterogeneous-horizon endogenous cancellation.** Agents incorporate a fraction `alpha`
  of anticipated aggregate impact; horizon averaging and impact feedback cancel the rough common
  signal. Independently measured `alpha` should match the Hawkes branching ratio, and the horizon
  density near zero should determine the remaining price regularity.
- **H0 -- latent decomposition and smoothing equivalence.** The common signal, martingale residual,
  agent horizons, forecast-to-position scale, impact kernel, and Hawkes offspring interpretation are
  not separately observed. Multiple latent mechanisms produce the same aggregate position and
  price law, while regularity saturation erases the proposed horizon signature.

### Discriminating result

A valid positive result would require an externally measured common forecast, participant-level
positions with declared decision horizons, an independently identified causal impact kernel, and a
branching ratio estimated from a separately valid event model. It would then test the frozen
equality and the regularity implication on an untouched period. A null result would be equally
valuable because it would refute the proposed balance mechanism without redefining latent
components after seeing price paths.

No current public EcoMD bridge supplies that joint contract. Price and anonymous trades alone do
not implement the intervention or expose the behavioral variable `alpha`.

## 3. What the new source proves

The observed price is decomposed as

\[
  S_t^\alpha=M_t+P_t+I_t^\alpha,
  \qquad
  P_t=\int_0^t K(t-s)\,dB_s,
\]

where `M` is a continuous martingale, `P` is a common predictable Gaussian--Volterra signal, and
impact is generated by the aggregate position `Pi` through

\[
  I_t^\alpha=G(0)\Pi_t^\alpha+
  \int_0^tG'(t-s)\Pi_s^\alpha\,ds.
\]

A continuum of agents is indexed by forecast horizon `lambda` with population law `nu`. Agent
positions are conditional forecasts of the signal increment plus an `alpha` fraction of the future
aggregate-impact increment. If the aggregate position has kernel `phi_alpha`, equilibrium is
equivalent to

\[
  (\mathrm{Id}-\alpha D_\nu\mathcal I)\phi_\alpha=D_\nu K,
\]

where `D_nu` is the average horizon-difference operator and `I` is the impact operator. The paper
constructs a unique decaying solution under an explicit contraction condition, while also giving an
exponentially growing resonance showing nonuniqueness in a broader admissible class.

The signal-driven price kernel can be written

\[
  \mathcal K_\alpha=A(\alpha,G(0))K+R_\alpha,
  \qquad
  A(\alpha,x)=\frac{1-(1-\alpha)x}{1+\alpha x}.
\]

The direct rough term cancels exactly when

\[
  G(0)(1-\alpha)=1.
\]

For a rough signal with exponent `H < 1/2` and Gamma horizons with shape `xi`, the uncancelled price
retains exponent `H`. Under cancellation, horizon averaging raises the signal-driven regularity;
above `xi > 1/2-H`, the full observed price is compatible with Brownian regularity. The Gamma rate
changes the horizon scale but not that local threshold.

The Hawkes comparison is a secondary identification. A dimensionless order-flow propagator has
instantaneous value `1/(1-||h||_1)`. Equating that entire dimensionless kernel to `G` turns the
cancellation equation into `alpha=||h||_1`. The paper does not estimate either behavioral horizons
or `alpha`, contains no empirical or simulation study, and does not claim that an anonymous market
tape independently identifies the equality.

## 4. Exact horizon nonidentification inside the model

The inverse-learning escape fails before noise, finite samples, or neural approximation. Consider
the valid submodel

\[
  \alpha=0,\qquad G(t)\equiv1,\qquad K(t)=e^{-rt},\quad r>0.
\]

It satisfies the cancellation condition `G(0)(1-alpha)=1`. Define the Laplace moment

\[
  m_\nu(r)=\int_0^\infty e^{-r\lambda}\,\nu(d\lambda).
\]

Because the horizon-shift operator acts on the exponential eigenfunction as

\[
  S_\nu K=m_\nu(r)K,
\]

the equilibrium equation gives

\[
  \phi_0=D_\nu K=(m_\nu(r)-1)K.
\]

Constant impact gives `I phi_0 = phi_0`, so the signal-driven price kernel is

\[
  \mathcal K_0=K+\phi_0=m_\nu(r)K.
\]

Thus, even if `B`, the aggregate position, and the signal-driven price were all observed without
error, their entire joint Gaussian process depends on `nu` only through the single number
`m_nu(r)`. Infinitely many distinct horizon distributions have the same value. Within the Gamma
family,

\[
  m_\nu(r)=\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta+r}\right)^\xi,
\]

so one observed moment leaves a continuum of different `(xi,beta)` pairs. Adding a neural density,
a differentiable fixed-point layer, or more optimizer steps cannot recover information absent from
the observation map.

This counterexample does not say that every sufficiently rich known signal kernel is
non-identifying. It proves the stronger gate-relevant fact that global identification over the
paper's stated model class is false. A positive inverse result would have to state an excitation
class, an injectivity or coercivity condition, the independently observed channels, and a stable
finite-sample theorem. The generic mean-field inverse problem is already known to be ill-posed and
its identifiable subspace characterized by operator coercivity, so merely regularizing a neural
inverse is not a new result.

## 5. Price regularity does not rescue the mechanism

The local exponent is a many-to-one diagnostic. Away from cancellation, all admissible Gamma shapes
retain the signal exponent `H`. Under cancellation and below the threshold, regularity becomes
`H+xi`; above the threshold, a nonzero finite kernel limit yields exponent one half. Therefore every
`xi > 1/2-H` can produce the same Brownian-compatible headline, and the martingale residual already
has that regularity. The Gamma rate `beta` is absent from the local criterion.

Consequently, Brownian-compatible price paths cannot jointly establish cancellation, identify the
horizon distribution, and validate the equality with Hawkes endogeneity. Estimating a branching
ratio from the same tape does not fix this: [Filimonov and Sornette
(2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6756) construct regime-mixture processes with true branching
ratio zero that calibrate near one and document timestamp and kernel-specification sensitivity.
This is exactly the repository's closed core-versus-reaction observation quotient.

## 6. Finite-population and solver rescues

### 6.1 Finite agents near cancellation

Replacing `S_nu` by an empirical horizon operator `S_nu_N` creates an order `N^{-1/2}` fluctuation
under ordinary conditions. The equilibrium error is then amplified by the resolvent of
`Id-alpha D_nu I`; under singular impact scaling it can also inherit factors proportional to
`1/(1-alpha)`. A double limit in which `N` grows while `alpha` approaches one could therefore fail
to commute.

That observation is a useful numerical warning, not yet an ICLR contribution. Functional central
limit theorems for finite-player mean-field games already characterize `sqrt(N)` equilibrium
fluctuations, including common noise, and generic operator perturbation gives the condition-number
amplification. Stratified or quasi-Monte-Carlo horizon quadrature and adaptive sample allocation are
standard repairs. No new lower bound, correction, or uniform-in-the-singular-limit theorem was
derived here.

EcoMD has an additional semantic failure: its particle count is a simulator resolution. Splitting
one latent agent into weighted replicas can preserve every aggregate market path while changing
`N` and an apparent finite-size exponent. The new paper supplies neither a market-native population
unit nor a split-invariant response, so it does not reopen
`fluctuation_aware_population_size_transfer`.

### 6.2 Resonance-aware differentiable equilibrium solving

The source's exponential homogeneous mode is a legitimate equilibrium-selection warning. But the
paper itself exhibits the resonance and restores uniqueness by selecting a decaying Banach space.
Detecting a small singular value, constraining stability, or unrolling the fixed point is standard
implicit-layer and system-identification practice. Without a new observable certificate and a
lower bound showing that existing stability-constrained solvers cannot suffice, this is a numerical
application rather than an irreducible learning method.
