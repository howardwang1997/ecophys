## Two ordinary observation controls

**Bulk and surface are different averages.** In a two-compartment illustration, let `f` be the sampled fraction belonging to a surface region and `z_s,z_b` its surface and bulk oxidized fractions. A bulk-weighted observation is `z_obs=f*z_s+(1-f)*z_b`. With metallic interior `z_b=0`, oxidized surface `z_s=1` and small `f`, the observation can remain predominantly metallic. Without a sampling kernel and detection bound, a bulk-metal description does not refute a surface-oxide result. This is a standard mixture identity, not a claim that a particular experiment missed a signal.

**The same endpoint permits different histories.** On `0≤t≤T`, the illustrative paths `s1(t)=t/T` and `s2(t)=(t/T)^2` have the same initial and final values but different integrals, `T/2` and `T/3`. An endpoint image alone cannot specify an arbitrary reaction-history functional. This is an observation-map counterexample, not a physically realized Cu trajectory; a justified kinetic model can impose further constraints. Conversely, an endpoint can still support endpoint-state prediction or other explicitly restricted estimands.

These controls are not new research questions, molecular discoveries or novel measurement methods. They prevent an apparent literature disagreement from being promoted into a topic. Neither finding is based on implementation errors or tool behaviour.
