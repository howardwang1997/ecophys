paper topics**. One crypto-ETP primary-market question remains F2-deferred; two broad questions

### Cheap checks for the non-escalated programs

For G19-02, moving a fixed expiry-related variance contribution from Thursday to Tuesday can
produce opposite weekday coefficients while leaving the sum of daily variances unchanged.
Weekday relocation alone therefore does not establish increased whole-week volatility.
The direct paper's indexed primary abstract concerns precisely the 2025 NIFTY/SENSEX weekday
reassignment, activity and volatility; its unpublished-paper status and unread full methods are
not concealed. This is a collision check, not an endorsement of its estimates.

For G19-04, let the closed underlying's last quote be 100 and new public information imply a
next-opening value of 102. A token moving to 102 has a two-unit gap from the stale quote while
performing useful price discovery. A gap alone cannot identify tracking failure. Existing
primary work already studies off-hours trading and price discovery; no new data test is warranted
for that broad formulation.

For G19-05, official documents date the trial to **September 8, 2026**, for at least 18 months.

reporting phase also introduces operational practices. An information-treatment study would be
a different estimand requiring separate assignment and data screening. No fail-statistics
endpoint or participant report was queried.

For G19-06, the circular distinguishes new contracts generated on the revised schedule from

## G19-03: exact wheel example and parent collision

### Protocol state and analytic result

The OCC document currently hosted at [S7], last revised October 9, 2007, describes an ordered
wheel of short positions, grouped by account/subaccount, with a random starting point,
25-contract increments and an intervening skip rule. The documented standard procedure excludes
classes using pro-rata assignment. Current production applicability, RNG semantics and all
class exceptions would need a versioned contract before any field or implementation claim.

Consider the explicitly declared uniform-start circular interpretation, with 50 short contracts,
25 exercises, and account A holding 25 contiguous wheel positions. There is only one assignment
increment, so the skip arithmetic does not enter. Let K be the overlap between A's positions
and the randomly rotated 25-position assignment block. Exactly one of the 50 starts gives each
of K=0 and K=25, and two starts give each K from 1 through 24. Thus

\[
P(K=0)=P(K=25)=1/50,\qquad P(K=k)=2/50\quad(1\leq k\leq24),
\]
\[
E[K]=25/2,\quad
E[K^2]=(25^2+2\sum_{k=1}^{24}k^2)/50=417/2,\quad
\operatorname{Var}(K)=209/4=52.25.
\]

The uniform **without-replacement** benchmark draws 25 of the 50 contracts. Its count for A is
hypergeometric, with the same mean and

\[
\operatorname{Var}(K_{\rm unif})=
25(1/2)(1/2)\frac{25}{49}=625/196.
\]

The variance ratio is therefore

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(K)}{\operatorname{Var}(K_{\rm unif})}
=\frac{10241}{625}=16.3856.
\]

This is an exact illustrative calculation, **not a measured 16.39-fold increase in market
risk**. Uniform sampling at a fixed total is itself dependent; it must not be called independent
Bernoulli assignment. Across all accounts, exactly 25 contracts are assigned, so aggregate
linear delivery is fixed. Account-level variance does not establish aggregate liquidity loss,
collateral shortfall or a price response. Those require portfolios, hedges and a specified
nonlinear funding/execution rule. Changing account grouping changes labelled protocol state;
it is not a harmless representation change or a verified trader-controlled intervention.

### Second-stage twin

OCC-to-member assignment and member-to-customer allocation are different kernels [S8]. Suppose
a broker receives exactly 25 assignments against 50 customer shorts, with two customers holding
25 each. A declared uniform random customer allocation gives a hypergeometric count. A declared
FIFO policy with all of A's positions first gives A exactly 25 assignments. The first-stage
broker total is identical. This conditional example shows why the broker total and the word
"random" cannot identify customer exposure. It does not establish the allocation policy of any
particular broker or assert that all approved random policies are uniform.

## G19-01: crypto-ETP delivery route remains unresolved

The SEC permitted in-kind creations and redemptions for relevant crypto ETPs on July 29, 2025
[S1]. Permission is not mandatory adoption or an assigned delivery method for each transaction.
The same announcement contains other approvals; fund-specific concurrent changes must be mapped
before attributing an observed response to delivery mode.

### Two analytic identification checks

**Delivery-label twin.** Redeem q underlying units. In an in-kind route the fund transfers q
units to the AP, which sells a fraction theta externally. In a directed-cash route, the fund
sells q units to that AP for pq while paying pq to redeem its shares. The two opposing cash
legs cancel; the AP receives the same q units and can make the same external sale theta*q.
Thus outside flow can be identical under opposite delivery labels. This frictionless twin does
not assert equality of financing, legal, settlement or tax costs. It shows why "cash" cannot
by itself imply external exchange selling. The SEC-hosted issuer schematic [S2] and the Central
Bank of Ireland's directed-cash discussion [S3, Schedule A, paragraph 20 and note 218] make this
distinction concrete. The schematic is illustrative 2023 material, not a complete 2025 operating
contract.

**Selection twin.** Let latent AP inventory capacity U affect both chosen delivery method D
and outside execution Y. The observational equation Y=beta*D+gamma*U+epsilon does not identify
beta when U is omitted and D is selected using U. An association between adoption and liquidity
can reflect route causation, AP selection or both. A common permission date does not remove
that ambiguity. Daily fund flows and market returns cannot distinguish the cash-leg twin or
recover this omitted state simply by adding more days.

The F2 neighborhood is [S1–S6]. Malamud already models AP-mediated primary/secondary liquidity
spillovers, including non-monotonic underlying effects. Chau et al. model endogenous cash/basket
choice with AP inventory and information frictions. The Bank of Canada note explains useful
annual N-CEN disclosures for its fixed-income fund sample; those fields cannot be assumed to
apply to commodity-trust ETPs or to reveal intraday AP execution. These are adjacent parents,
not an exact-paper novelty closure of the July 2025 crypto permission.

**F2 deferred, not active or novelty-qualified.** The next decisive update is an outcome-blind
schema/rights inventory establishing actual transaction mode and joined execution or financing
state, plus defensible assignment. Stop if the available source is only daily fund flow,
permission dates or annual aggregate mode shares. Preserve the two-route cash-flow contract;
do not replace the empirical question with a generic ETF liquidity simulation. No dependency
on Paper E or Paper F results has been identified.
