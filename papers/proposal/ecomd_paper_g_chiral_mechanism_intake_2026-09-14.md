The intake identifies concrete controls, but no same-reaction unresolved fork or distinctive learning task. Stop generic nonlinear-effect curve diagnosis as a new formulation; asymmetric catalysis remains open.

[Ali, Blackmond and Bures (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.2c00783) already construct nonlinear enantiomeric-excess effects from a monomeric catalytic cascade and propose catalyst-concentration/rate-order measurements to help distinguish models. This is a direct parent to the proposed diagnostic intervention. First-order behavior is not, by itself, a universal proof of monomeric catalysis.

[Geiger et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1039/D0SC04724D) provide experimental loading- and ligand-ee-dependent enantiodivergence in NME-catalyzed dimethylzinc addition, with a competing monomer/dimer model. This is a different native reaction from both the cascade and copper systems; similar or opposite curve shapes do not establish contradictory predictions for one reaction.

[Zhu et al. (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-57380-x) combine loading and ligand/metal-ratio interventions, isotope-labelled ligand mass spectrometry, oxidation comparisons, structural evidence and calculations in copper carboesterification. Their QMS protocol measures a pre-coordinated mixture after 200-fold dilution into acetonitrile. Its mapping to reaction-state speciation therefore needs explicit qualification. This observation is not evidence that dilution invalidates their result. A multi-ligand, mononuclear species is not a multi-metal aggregate.

A standard constructive control tests a proposed correlation diagnostic. Let A_R and A_S be a turnover-active enantiomer pair, and X_R and X_S a rapidly exchanging off-cycle pair satisfying

X_R=q A_R, X_S=q A_S, q>0.

Then ee_X=ee_A. Under equal mirror turnover rates and opposite intrinsic selectivities ±epsilon, steady product selectivity satisfies ee_product=epsilon ee_A=epsilon ee_X. Both measured pools therefore exhibit the same linear correlation with the product, although only A directly turns over substrate. Loading or feed-ee changes that preserve this symmetric relation do not distinguish the pools. This is a generic limiting model, not a new theorem or a chemically qualified alternative for the copper reaction. The latter paper uses additional evidence, which this control does not refute.

[Singh and Hernandez-Lobato (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58854-8) supply an adjacent ML parent: few-example selectivity prediction with pressure, temperature and catalyst loading included. The discussed binary high-selectivity task differs from signed enantiodivergence and active-species identification. An untested extrapolation of that model cannot be presented as a failure, and simply adding concentration features is not new.

A defensible future learning task must specify what independently distinguishes the mechanistic states, rather than using an ee correlation as its own mechanism label. It must also hold substrate/network, ligand coordination, nuclearity, conversion, clock and observation protocol explicit.
