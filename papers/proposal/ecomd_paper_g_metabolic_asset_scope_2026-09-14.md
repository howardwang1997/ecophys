## Primary evidence and public metadata

[MetaFlowTrain, Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58530-x)
separates organisms while allowing downstream metabolite flow. The selected experiments
include reversed bacterial/fungal chamber orders and mixed controls, plus an upstream
wild-type versus triple-mutant producer comparison. Community measurements include
62-hour endpoints; selected metabolite sampling also includes 24 hours. This is meaningful
physical control, not merely a device proposal. However, the reviewed sections do not
establish a dense library of matched inlet/outlet histories across flow and composition
interventions for learning a transferable chamber response. The authors report randomized
condition placement across media/pumps and separate trains as replicates. Reading scope:
selected platform, experimental design, measurement and availability sections, not a
complete supplement or outcome-data audit.

[Metabolic blueprints of monocultures, January 2026 preprint](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41648583/)
uses growth and longitudinal exometabolite measurements from 15 rhizosphere strains
to fit a consumer-resource model and predict multi-strain communities. Selected indexed
primary methods describe defined NLDM medium, batch cultures, and species-specific
consumption/transformation parameters. Thus predicting communities from monocultures
with metabolite-mediated models already has a direct parent. It is not a matched
comparison against the chamber experiment: organisms, medium and transport regime differ.
Read the abstract and selected indexed model/measurement sections; not a full review.

The official [MetaFlowTrain repository](https://github.com/gchesneau53/Script_from_Chesneau_et_al_2025)
lists figure-organized scripts/tables. Its README says the processed untargeted table
for Figure 2 panels C/D is available on request; the paper also identifies raw mass
spectrometry and sequencing archives. These are different deliverables: a raw archive
does not automatically supply a frozen processed feature matrix and sample join.

The official [metaCRM repository](https://github.com/segrelab/metaCRM_SynCom) lists
processed monoculture measurements, experimental community tables, fitted parameters

file, model, notebook or script was loaded or run. A repository license marker does

## Two standard controls retained for future screening

**C1 — fixed chamber maps can produce order effects.** Let concentration maps be
T_A(c)=c/2+1 and T_B(c)=c/4 for c>=0. Then

\[
T_B(T_A(c))=c/8+1/4,\qquad T_A(T_B(c))=c/8+1.
\]

The difference is -3/4 although both maps are fixed, nonnegative affine transformations.
Thus order-dependent output does not alone identify physiological adaptation, a
new higher-order biological interaction or a failure of modular composition. A serious
future test must compare predictions of the composed fixed maps under common input/state
support. These maps are a mathematical control, not fitted microbial transformations.

**C2 — pooled concentration does not determine nonlinear exposure.** For duration T,
compare c(t)=1 throughout with c(t)=0 in the first half and 2 in the second. Both have
mean concentration 1. If a fixed illustrative growth law obeys d log N/dt=c(t)^2,
the log-growth increments are T and 2T respectively. This difference requires no
adaptive cellular memory. Endpoints or pooled samples do not automatically specify
the time-dependent input needed for predictive composition. The quadratic law is
an illustrative control, not a biological assertion or inferred measurement model.

These are neither new theorems nor proposed stand-alone topics. They strengthen the
information needed to discriminate future mechanisms without manufacturing an ML claim.
