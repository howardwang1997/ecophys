## G20-01: retain the arithmetic, close the novelty pitch

Current rules build each directed pair's reference from single-pair bids and discard a batch
if its pair score is lower. Winning bids cannot overlap on a directed pair [S1]. In the
following **abstract score-level fixture**, there is one order on each of two token-disjoint
pairs, no hooks, fixed score conversion and costs, and compatible singleton settlements.
Scores include applicable costs. No production EVM feasibility or equilibrium is claimed;
the example is conditional on these executable offers being available.

| Offer | Pair 1 score | Pair 2 score | Available initially? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Single 1 | 1 | 0 | Yes |
| Single 2 | 0 | 1 | Yes |
| Batch | 50 | 50 | Yes |
| New single 1 | 51 | 0 | No |

Initially the reference is (1,1), so the batch is eligible and wins with total score 100.
Adding the last offer changes the reference to (51,1). The batch fails on pair 1; the two
compatible singletons give 52. This requires neither tie-breaking nor an approximate
optimizer. The 100-to-52 comparison is a declared arithmetic example, **not an observed
48% market loss or a prediction about entry equilibrium**. If the reference were held fixed,
old eligible allocations would remain feasible and exact maximum-score selection would be
weakly monotone. The changed eligibility constraint is the relevant distinction.

This is not a new mechanism theorem. Canidio and Henneke explicitly analyze aggressive
individual bids that disqualify rivals' batches and strategic responses to that possibility
[S2, Section 5]. A solver raised the same batch-exclusion concern in the April 2025 CIP-67
discussion; the response discusses separate offers and the cost allocation rationale [S3].
We have not established that this particular numerical fixture appears there, and do not
need that stronger claim to close the generic novelty pitch. Solver rewards [S5] also do not
turn a fixed-bid deletion statistic into an equilibrium intervention. No reward manipulation,
bid submission, API outcome call or expanded solver simulation is performed.

## G20-03: the deadline map is sharper than a single treatment date

The May 14, 2024 Clearstream notice announces a June 8 service change, separately from the
US settlement transition on May 28. It specifies an old 15:00 cutoff with same-day recalls
due T+2, and a new 19:30 cutoff with same-day recalls due T+1; later notices roll into the
next business day [S6]. These are the source's **CET-labelled service times**, not verified
UTC offsets or proof of actual deployment.

For an eligible business-day notification at service-clock time u, excluding equality at
the cutoffs and assuming unchanged calendars, the announced due-day offsets are

\[
d_0(u)=2+\mathbf1\{u>15{:}00\},\qquad
d_1(u)=1+\mathbf1\{u>19{:}30\}.
\]

| Notification interval | Old offset | New offset | Due date advanced by |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Before 15:00 | 2 business days | 1 business day | 1 business day |
| Between 15:00 and 19:30 | 3 business days | 1 business day | 2 business days |
| After 19:30 | 3 business days | 2 business days | 1 business day |

Thus the middle interval has one additional business day of contractual compression.
This is an algebraic consequence of the announced schedule, not an empirical finding,
new economic theorem or measured change in available cash. June 8 is the announced service
availability date, not an assumed observation date; eligibility, holidays, weekend handling,
cutoff equality and actual activation must be pinned before constructing treatment rows.

The native fork concerns **actual borrower pressure**. The notice describes lender
substitution before a borrower recall. Receipt of a lender's instruction, generation of a
borrower notice, its contractual due date and actual return are therefore different events.
Same-day notifications can be strategically timed: existing securities-lending research
models the joint lending/trading/recall choice [S8]. A policy clock does not randomize that
choice. General industry best-practice guidance is voluntary [S7], and cannot substitute for
the operator's exact historical rule or deployment record.

The minimum next check is source-only and outcome-blind. It must establish a lawful event
asset with instruction/receipt/recall timestamps, loan identity, substitution and partial
return lineage, security calendars, contractual due date, actual return/failure and coverage
of uncensored loans. It must also establish actual rollout and a credible assignment or
sorting-control argument. A future cross-time-band comparison is only a proposed design;
continuity, no timing manipulation and absence of differential concurrent changes are
unverified. A same-estimand independent replication, release rights, untouched confirmation
partition and cost estimate remain open. Public prices or daily lending totals do not by
themselves close these joins. No broader public-data impossibility is proved here.

The preliminary, non-authorizing record is
`research/paper_g/clearstream_recall_source_screen_20260908.yaml`. Stop this formulation if
the required event lifecycle cannot be lawfully recovered or the proposed time-band contrast
cannot separate sorting from a deadline response. Do not substitute a simulator-generated
effect, a fee change or aggregate T+1 performance to rescue it.

### F2 contract, second twin and neighborhood

Only G20-03 received the expanded screen. Its six primary anchors are the operator notice
and industry guidance [S6–S7], the endogenous-recall model [S8], RD identification and
falsification theory [S9], the official reporting timetable [S10], and the dividend-induced
stock-loan/liquidity study [S11]. The latter already occupies a broad claim that lending
constraints affect equity liquidity. Its dividend/tax intervention is not the same treatment
as a recall cutoff, so it is neither an exact cutoff collision nor a contrary prediction.
The bounded exact-cutoff search did not establish a matching paper; that is insufficient to
establish novelty. No fifteen-work audit or forecast was opened.

| Contract element | Exact proposed scope and present qualification |
|---|---|
| X: native state | Pre-existing eligible loans, lender instructions, notification times, party links, inventories and substitution capacity, calendars and actual service version. Required state is named; no joined asset is established. |
| do(A): legal action | The operator's joint cutoff/return-term rule change. The time-band interaction is proposed treatment heterogeneity, not an independently randomized cutoff or an identified unbundled coefficient. |
| Y: response | Borrower return fraction over a common 24-business-hour horizon, with the risk set fixed before substitution; linked cover trades or financing costs would be required to infer financial pressure. A changed failure rate defined against a changed due date is not sufficient. |
| H1:H0: separating prediction | Earlier borrower returns/cover versus substitution that leaves borrower pressure unchanged. Retain substituted and non-notified loans; conditioning only on borrower notices would select on a post-treatment event. |
| T: truth contract | Announced calendar arithmetic is known conditionally. Actual deployment, source-clock history, pre-state/event coverage, selection control, joint rights and replication remain unqualified. No data or scientific execution is authorized. |

For a second, analytic selection twin, let recorded relative notification time U be uniform
on (-1,1), and let Z=1{U>0} label one side of a cutoff. In world A, Y(z)=z. In world B, draw
H as a fair binary variable, then draw U uniformly on (0,1) if H=1 and (-1,0) otherwise;
set Y(z)=H for both z. Both worlds have the same uniform time density and observed Y=Z,
but their intervention effects are one and zero. A notification-density continuity check
alone cannot distinguish them. This standard identification counterexample is not a model
of actual Clearstream clients or a new impossibility theorem. It explains why clock rules
and visually smooth arrivals do not replace the continuity/randomization assumptions [S9].

FINRA currently schedules SLATE launch for September 28, 2028 [S10]. That future facility
cannot supply the missing 2024 event panel today; nor has a historical backfill or complete
substitution/borrower-cover schema been established. This preserves cycle18's existing
source boundary. It does not establish that every private operator or vendor asset is
unavailable. Of the program's weakest links, actual joined truth and assignment are decisive;
conditional discriminative value cannot compensate for them. No sample size, runtime or
publication probability is invented. The next source-only check must resolve those links
before additional modeling or a full novelty audit has value.
