Two standard controls clarify what an intervention can establish:

1. For an unconstrained classical Hamiltonian H_M(q,p)=U(q)+p^T M^-1 p/2 with a fixed coordinate-independent positive mass matrix, momentum integration yields rho_M(q) proportional to exp(-beta U(q)). The determinant factor from M cancels on normalization. A physical mass change can therefore preserve the canonical coordinate ensemble while changing time evolution. Coordinate freezing does not preserve that ensemble. This argument does not automatically extend to nuclear quantum equilibrium, constrained coordinate measures or finite, unequilibrated glass trajectories. Auxiliary cell inertia must also be separated from atomic mass.
2. Consider a passive gate switching closed/open at rate gamma in each direction, with absorption at rate k only when open. The unabsorbed gate has stationary open probability 1/2 at all gamma. The backward equations yield T_open=2/k and T_closed=2/k+1/gamma. Starting from the stationary gate mixture gives mean first passage 2/k+1/(2 gamma). Thus changing gate speed changes passage time even with the same stationary gate distribution and no explicit propulsive motion. This is not a bulk conductivity law, isotope calculation or calibrated electrolyte model; gamma has not been identified with an atomic mass.

The controls are elementary, not new theorems. They prevent mass sensitivity or constrained-motion effects from being treated as unique evidence for one mechanism. A future method needs different predictions from named mechanisms under the same physical intervention, together with a useful learning gain over existing event/trajectory methods. Merely changing event labels, adding a classifier or observing a rate change does not meet that requirement.
