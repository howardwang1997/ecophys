## Question actually screened

`g39_physiological_memory_sequence_advantage` — `empirical_intervention`; source lane `market_native_action_or_constraint` under the cross-domain scope revision.

Native object: a declared bacterial strain/population and its measured response to an ordered sequence of antibiotic exposures. The research question is whether an induced physiological state contributes to sequence-dependent susceptibility beyond selection among resistance genotypes. The intervention is the exposure order/history and recovery interval; the response is a declared survival/growth trajectory and subsequent adaptation, kept distinct rather than pooled into one endpoint.

- H1: induced physiological state materially changes the response to the next environment, even when inherited resistance is accounted for.
- H0: the tested sequence effect is adequately explained by genetic composition and contemporaneous exposure, with no incremental induced-state contribution.
- Discriminator: within a fixed strain background, compare histories with matched subsequent challenge and relevant pre-state measurements, and contrast the corresponding genotype-only and physiology-aware predictions. A wash is not by itself a complete pre-state match.
- Positive value: identify a state variable that a sequence predictor/control model must represent. Null value: specify regimes where genotype/composition-based prediction is sufficient. Neither result automatically supplies a new learning algorithm.

These are nested explanatory alternatives, not mutually exclusive biological processes. The question was source-informed; no prospective scientific forecast is asserted. No same-estimand disagreement between two publications was established.

## Two standard analytic controls

**C1: state augmentation is necessary in the stated toy, not a new method.** Let the response be `r(g,a,h)=r0(g,a)+k(a)h`, where `g` is inherited state, `a` current exposure and `h` induced physiological state. Two histories with the same `g,a` and different `h` can have different response. A genotype-only state is then insufficient, but a state-space or partially observed model is an ordinary parent construction. This toy does not demonstrate a new biological effect or identify `h` from growth alone.

**C2: a recovery curve need not identify its molecular cause.** In an illustrative observation model, `y(t)=k x0 exp(-lambda t)` can be generated either by a decaying bound-target state or by a decaying autonomous stress state with matching parameters. Washes that affect only a separate free-drug compartment leave these two explanations observationally equivalent. Fitting the recovery curve more accurately does not resolve that equivalence. This is not a claim that either single-exponential model describes the organism; mixtures and coupling remain possible. The primary authors already acknowledge the bound-drug distinction.

The reusable result is a scope guardrail: distinguish predictive memory from identification of its molecular carrier. We do not create a second topic out of this generic nonidentifiability example or turn it into another calibration-transfer proposal.
