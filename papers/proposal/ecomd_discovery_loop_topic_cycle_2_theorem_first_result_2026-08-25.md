# EcoMD Discovery Loop topic cycle 2: theorem-first market-physics screen

**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Scope:** simulated markets, market physics, and financial physics
**Stage:** D-3/D-2 paper-only screening
**Literature cutoff:** 2026-08-25
**Outcome access:** none; no simulator output, market outcome, sealed split, paid asset, or sandbox result was opened
**Decision:** zero machine topic cards, zero D-1 candidates, and zero execution authorizations

## 1. Decision

The second bounded Discovery Loop reversed the usual order of ideation. Instead of starting
from a venue feature and attaching a physics analogy, it began with eight candidate theorem
objects: monotone coupling, an edit-stability radius, submodular liquidity response, integer
reachability, discrete funding control, coupled price lattices, liquidation chattering, and
machine-checked matching conformance. Each object was then required to survive an exact
market-state counterexample, a parent-problem reduction, and a same-estimand truth contract.

No object survived. Four broad claims are false without assumptions that effectively impose
the answer: universal no-passing in an adaptive book, universal submodularity of liquidity
addition, a coordinate-invariant cross-venue moiré phase, and universal liquidation
chattering. The other four reduce to established machinery: program sensitivity and
incremental discrete-event simulation, integer feasibility, feedback-control stability, and
formal verification of a continuous double auction.

The highest hostile-T0 upper bound was 7%, and every conservative lower bound was at most
1%. This round therefore creates no `candidate`, `parked`, `active`, or disposable sandbox.
It authorizes no implementation, simulation, outcome access, data download or purchase,
external outreach, EcoMD change, or GPU work.

## 2. Frozen theorem-first contract

Every worksheet still used the Discovery Loop object

\[
(X,\operatorname{do}(A),Y,H_1\!:\!H_0,T),
\]

but its claim had to include a mathematical quantifier before any empirical story:

- the class of allowed market states and event kernels;
- the order, metric, lattice, or control operator being claimed;
- the unit, clock, label, and event-refinement transformations under which it must persist;
- one exact finite counterexample and one parent-problem reduction; and
- a truth contract in two independent native implementations for the same object.

The frozen search strings were variants of `limit order book monotone coupling`, `matching
engine edit stability`, `liquidity intervention submodular`, `round lot integer market
reachability`, `perpetual funding feedback stability`, `coupled limit order books tick grid
synchronization`, `partial liquidation chattering`, and `verified continuous double
auction`. Inclusion required a primary paper, official protocol, or author/institutional
copy that fixed the relevant theorem or mechanism. Search snippets, review prose, and an
absence of a matching title could not support novelty.

### Falsification and nonclaim ledger

| Worksheet | Result that immediately closes the claim | Two explicit nonclaims |
|---|---|---|
| No-passing | one admissible adaptive kernel reverses or branches the proposed order within a finite horizon | not all strategies are attractive; L2 does not identify a coupled discrepancy |
| Stability radius | the bound changes under economically equivalent message splitting, or the affected set is the full trace | not a new replay algorithm; not an economic metric until refinement invariance is proved |
| Submodularity | one pair `A subset B` and order `e` violates diminishing returns | not an average empirical tendency; not a welfare claim |
| Inventory lattice | an economically equivalent contract split changes the certificate | not a prediction of realized prices; not a new integer-programming solver |
| Funding control | the central stability result is a direct corollary or exact prior, or an unbundled assignment lacks support | not a claim that funding always anchors basis; not identification from endogenous exchange changes |
| Tick lattice | the proposed phase changes under numeraire or contract rescaling, or ordinary arbitrage reproduces it | not a generic price-discovery study; not a claim that latency deviations are lattice phases |
| Liquidation chattering | positive-lot monotone exposure gives finite termination, or cycles vanish when reset inputs are frozen | not a crash-causality claim; landed liquidations are not the opportunity set |
| Verified matching | an existing verified checker returns the same certificate | not behavioral validation; not a market-dynamics law |

For universal theorem claims, one finite violation is the minimum scientifically meaningful
effect and terminates the worksheet. For constructive claims, the minimum effect was a
nonzero invariant or bound that survives event splitting, unit refinement, and at least one
fixed finite horizon in two native engines. None reached that constructive stage.

## 4. Worksheet 1 — one-order no-passing susceptibility

- **State `X`:** a full owner-labelled price-time book and an exogenous common arrival
  sequence, including all cancellation targets.
- **Intervention `do(A)`:** add one unit bid at a frozen price to one copy of the market.
- **Response `Y`:** the signed discrepancy between coupled books and the induced best-price
  difference over event time.
- **`H1`:** the discrepancy behaves as a market-native second-class particle obeying a
  no-passing order and yields a transferable susceptibility.
- **`H0`:** this is an ordinary attractive coupling available only for restricted exogenous
  LOB dynamics; strategic, state-dependent order generation destroys the partial order.
- **Truth `T`:** two engines would need a common event kernel and isolated common randomness,
  not merely matched seeds or aggregate L2 output.

Yudovina's simplified LOB already proves an exact one-extra-order monotonicity lemma under a
shared arrival process. Simatos already enriches an LOB with order filiation and couples it
to a branching random walk. Thus the restricted positive result is occupied. Conversely,
an adaptive cancellation policy may cancel the added order immediately, while an agent
conditioned on the improved quote may submit arbitrarily many descendants. Those two native
kernels start from the same one-unit perturbation and give bounded versus branching
discrepancy. There is no universal order on the adaptive-engine class.

**Close at 1/3/6%.** A child can reopen only with a new market-specific partial order that is
preserved by realistic cancellation and adaptive-agent kernels, is not Yudovina monotonicity
or Simatos branching in new notation, and has two native common-randomness implementations.

## 5. Worksheet 2 — dynamic matching stability radius

For deterministic engine `E`, event stream `theta`, observable `f`, and an event-edit metric
`d`, define

\[
\rho_f(E,\theta)=\inf\{d(\theta,\theta'):\;f(E(\theta'))\ne f(E(\theta))\}.
\]

- **State `X`:** the full event stream, matching rule, order IDs, priority and engine state.
- **Intervention `do(A)`:** one admissible insert, delete, quantity, price, or timing edit.
- **Response `Y`:** the first changed trade, price move, or fixed-horizon path functional.
- **`H1`:** market geometry yields a nontrivial local stability certificate and an affected
  cone substantially smaller than full replay.
- **`H0`:** the one-shot object is cumulative executable depth; the dynamic object is generic
  program sensitivity and incremental discrete-event computation.

For a frozen book and fixed aggressive order, the price-move radius is exactly a cumulative
depth or first-gap calculation. For a dynamic exogenous stream, Hanai et al.'s
exact-differential simulation already reprocesses changed events and all causal influences
while matching full re-execution. Self-adjusting computation supplies the corresponding
generic correctness semantics. With adaptive agents, one early fill can change every later
action, so the affected set can be the whole trace. Worse, split-versus-aggregate messages
alter edit distance without changing economic state, making an unqualified radius
representation-dependent.

**Close at 1/3/6%.** Reopen only with an event-refinement-invariant market metric and a
strictly market-specific upper/lower bound that is not cumulative depth, formal conformance,
exact-differential simulation, or generic change propagation.

## 6. Worksheet 3 — liquidity-intervention submodularity

Let `F(S)` be the reduction in a preregistered adverse price event after adding the set `S`
of admissible resting orders to a frozen book. The proposed theorem was diminishing returns,
`F(A+e)-F(A) >= F(B+e)-F(B)` for `A subset B`.

The universal statement is false in the smallest threshold book. Choose two one-unit
liquidity additions `a,b` and an incoming order such that neither addition alone prevents a
two-level penetration but both together do. Then

\[
F(\varnothing)=F(\{a\})=F(\{b\})=0,\qquad F(\{a,b\})=1,
\]

which violates submodularity. Changing the response to immediately executable quantity can
make it modular or concave, but then it is only cumulative depth. With strategic future
flow, a market kernel can encode arbitrary complementarity or substitution. Assumptions
strong enough to recover submodularity therefore impose the desired response structure;
standard submodular optimization would then be the parent method.

**Close at 0/2/5%.** Reopen only if a native exchange rule forces a nontrivial submodular
quantity despite the threshold twin, with a proof stable to order splitting and an external
same-estimand intervention. Do not estimate a submodularity ratio from selected simulator
paths and treat it as a law.

## 7. Worksheet 4 — integer inventory-lattice accessibility

- **State `X`:** integer positions/cash and the protocol's integer action vectors, lot sizes,
  fees, caps and collateral inequalities.
- **Intervention `do(A)`:** enable one native trade, liquidation, or transfer action.
- **Response `Y`:** whether a target safe set is reachable and the minimum transient
  inventory/capital barrier.
- **`H1`:** a lattice index or torsion class is a market-physics obstruction to liquidity or
  solvency recovery.
- **`H0`:** equality reachability is integer linear algebra; inequalities and capacities are
  ordinary integer programming.

With reversible integer action matrix `A`, equality reachability of `y-x` is exactly
membership in the lattice generated by `A`, computable through standard integer normal
forms. Adding direction, capacity, margin, or path constraints yields integer or
resource-constrained reachability, already native to combinatorial market clearing. The
putative physical index also fails a unit-refinement twin: splitting one contract into `m`
economically equivalent subcontracts changes the coordinate lattice and its index. If the
certificate is normalized to economic exposure so that the twin is invariant, the apparent
torsion disappears or becomes the original integer-feasibility condition.

**Close at 1/3/6%.** Reopen only with a protocol-induced invariant under economically
equivalent contract refinement and a theorem that provably does not reduce to integer linear
algebra, mixed-integer optimization, or previously closed action-grammar reachability.

## 8. Worksheet 5 — perpetual-funding controller oscillation

- **State `X`:** perpetual basis, index, open interest, positions, funding accumulator,
  sampling interval, cap/clamp/deadband, liquidation and arbitrage state.
- **Intervention `do(A)`:** change one frozen feedback coefficient or update interval.
- **Response `Y`:** basis stability, overshoot, funding tails and liquidation probability.
- **`H1`:** discrete sampling creates a new market-specific oscillatory phase boundary.
- **`H0`:** it is sampled-data feedback with saturation and delay, plus strategic demand.

Zhang already models funding as an algorithmic feedback rule, derives a stability condition
and welfare-optimal strength, and analyzes update intervals, caps, clamps and
liquidation-driven crashes. Kim and Park derive funding designs that anchor perpetual prices
and analyze path dependence. Hence both the control interpretation and the central design
axes are directly occupied. Existing dYdX governance assignments also failed the project's
support gate, so a rephrased controller story cannot reopen that field route.

**Close at 0/1/3%.** Reopen only with a new theorem forced by an unmodeled native mechanism
and a prospective, unbundled assignment with adequate support in at least two independently
governed systems.

## 9. Worksheet 6 — cross-venue tick-lattice phase locking

- **State `X`:** two books for the same economic asset, tick/lot/contract multipliers,
  fees, FX conversion, latencies, router inventory and message clocks.
- **Intervention `do(A)`:** change one venue's tick while holding all other economics fixed.
- **Response `Y`:** duration and magnitude of cross-venue price deviations or phase slips.
- **`H1`:** commensurate versus incommensurate price lattices cause a moiré-like locking law.
- **`H0`:** finite currency ticks share a rational refinement; deviations are arbitrage with
  transaction costs, queueing, latency and inventory constraints.

Exchange prices are finite-precision rational quantities after conversion to a common
numeraire. Two ticks therefore lie on a common integer refinement determined by their
rational greatest common unit; there is no irrational incommensurability in the deployed
state space. Rescaling a contract or stock unit can also change the apparent phase while
leaving economic payoffs unchanged. Once fees, asynchronous clocks and limited arbitrage are
added, the remaining object is established fragmented-market synchronization and coupled-LOB
dynamics. Tick-size field studies already show that fragmentation and fee structure govern
cross-market price discovery.

**Close at 1/3/7%.** Reopen only with a unit/numeraire-invariant observable whose law cannot
be reproduced by ordinary latency-and-inventory arbitrage, plus a clean one-tick assignment
and two complete synchronized L3 systems.

## 10. Worksheet 7 — partial-liquidation chattering

- **State `X`:** account debt/collateral, oracle and AMM state, minimum liquidation lot,
  close factor, bonus/fees, block clock and admissible re-risking actions.
- **Intervention `do(A)`:** change only the close factor or minimum partial-liquidation lot.
- **Response `Y`:** count and spacing of liquidation episodes before safety or bankruptcy.
- **`H1`:** a discrete market creates a chattering boundary analogous to a nonsmooth hybrid
  system.
- **`H0`:** monotone partial reduction terminates finitely; recurrent crossings require
  external oracle motion, re-risking or replenishment and are generic threshold dynamics.

If every liquidation reduces risky exposure by at least the positive native lot `q_min`, a
position of size `q` admits at most `ceil(q/q_min)` such reductions before exhaustion. Thus
there is no Zeno accumulation in event or block time. If borrowing, price moves or collateral
changes reset the account, cycles are possible but are driven by the external policy and
ordinary hybrid reachability. Recent work already optimizes dynamic liquidation with fees
and derives closed-form bounds, while liquidation-mechanism research directly studies price
impact and participation.

**Close at 1/3/6%.** Reopen only with a protocol-native reset rule yielding a new invariant
cycle or lower bound under complete state, two exact implementations, and an assigned close-
factor treatment. Successful on-chain liquidations alone do not reveal the opportunity set.

## 11. Worksheet 8 — verified matching conformance

- **State `X`:** a complete order stream and a formal price-time or uniform-price matching
  specification.
- **Intervention `do(A)`:** mutate one order or claimed trade in a submitted log.
- **Response `Y`:** a proof-carrying violation witness, uniqueness certificate or corrected
  allocation.
- **`H1`:** a market-native verified checker is a new scientific truth engine for simulator
  discovery.
- **`H0`:** this is formal software conformance, valuable infrastructure but not a new
  financial-physics result.

Garg and Sarswat already provide an `O(n log n)` formally verified CDA, a matching lower
bound and an automatic checker. Their related multi-quantity work formalizes double-auction
algorithms, uniqueness results and real-exchange log checking. This is almost the proposed
object verbatim. It could be reused as a baseline or integrity oracle, but a checker does not
create a Nature-scale scientific question.

**Close at 0/1/2%.** Reopen only for a scientific theorem about market dynamics that uses a
verified engine as infrastructure; never claim conformance itself as market physics.

## 13. What this cycle changes

The useful result is not another blacklist of names. It adds a theorem-first fork to the
Discovery Loop:

1. **False-universal branch.** If a finite native counterexample violates the proposed law,
   close it before prior-art ranking or simulation. Submodularity and tick-moiré failed here.
2. **Imposed-assumption branch.** If the theorem holds only after assuming attractiveness,
   concavity, affine response, or a fixed exogenous trace, test whether that assumption is
   the entire result. No-passing failed here.
3. **Parent-exact branch.** Remove market nouns and reduce the object to program sensitivity,
   integer feasibility, sampled-data control, hybrid reachability, or formal conformance.
   If the reduction preserves inputs and outputs, close the formulation.
4. **Truth-capability branch.** Only after the first three branches survive should the search
   require two native engines and a prospective real bridge.

After two bounded cycles, the binding constraint is not idea count. It is the absence of a
qualified truth capability that jointly supplies a nontrivial intervention, complete state,
two independent same-estimand implementations, and a sealed external check. The next paper-
only cycle should therefore start from a newly available capability or theorem residual, not
from another analogy. Until such an input exists, generating more mechanism stories has low
expected information gain.
