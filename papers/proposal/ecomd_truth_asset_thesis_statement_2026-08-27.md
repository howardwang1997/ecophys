## The topic in one sentence

**Does randomizing the matching priority among equal-price resting orders (FIFO → uniform
random within the tie set) change the liquidity of a real market?** — adjudicated by a
preregistered controlled experiment that separates the two rival model families of market
microstructure.

## The rival explanations (the scientific fork)

- **H0 — exchangeable-population invariance (statistical queue family: zero-intelligence,
  queue-reactive models).** Order-level arrival and cancellation intensities depend only on
  aggregate book state, not on order identity or age. Under this assumption the aggregate
  market law is provably invariant to the allocation rule (Lemma 1, C2 document): spreads,
  depths, event counts, impact — everything anonymous — is unchanged by the rule; only
  *who* gets filled changes.
- **H1 — strategic queue-value response (market-maker family: Avellaneda–Stoikov and
  descendants).** Time priority grants fill-probability rents. Randomization removes the
  rent of arriving early, and the population responds: Lemma 2 proves the direction within
  the canonical threshold family — randomized priority sustains deeper touch queues; spread
  and repositioning responses are secondary predictions.

Both outcomes are publishable: H0 validates statistical-queue simulators for rule response
and falsifies strategic extrapolation; H1 validates adaptive simulators and demonstrates the
simulator-prediction gap (fitting passive paths does not determine rule response) on
assigned human-market truth.

## Why human participants are structurally necessary

The fork is precisely about whether a *population* responds strategically to a rule. Three
lines force the laboratory:

1. **Robots cannot adjudicate.** A robot population's response is whatever we injected:
  zero-intelligence robots satisfy H0 by construction, strategic robots satisfy H1 by
  construction. Simulator-versus-simulator comparison is circular — the recorded lesson of
  Cycles 8 and 16.
2. **Field data cannot adjudicate.** No exchange has ever randomized within-tie priority
  (C1 collision manifest); there is no natural experiment and no counterfactual.
3. **Humans are the only third-party truth.** Lemma 1's five failure channels — age-dependent
  cancellation hazards, trader heterogeneity, conditioning on queue composition, strategic
  repositioning, fairness perception (Perry & Zarsky's FIFO-fairness evidence) — are all
  human-population properties. Only a human market can violate H0.

Participants are therefore the adjudicating instrument, not an accessory: the estimand is
the response of an interacting human market population to one legal, assignable rule change.
