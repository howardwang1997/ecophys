## 2. The discriminating fork (stated before family dispositions)

**H0 — exchangeable-tie-set invariance (statistical queue family).** In queue-reactive models
whose order-level arrival and cancellation intensities condition only on aggregate book state
(spread, queue lengths, recent price moves) and not on order identity, tie-set members are
exchangeable. Under exchangeability, replacing FIFO allocation among equal-price resting orders
by a random draw changes only *which* member is filled; the induced law of every aggregate
observable (best quotes, touch depth, event counts) is **invariant** in law to the allocation
rule. In-silico representatives: zero-intelligence traders
([Gode and Sunder 1993](https://doi.org/10.1086/261849)) and the queue-reactive submodel of the
pinned Sfendourakis framework; the queue-position analysis of
[Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbt020) supplies the
identity-agnostic baseline.

**H1 — strategic queue-value response (adaptive family).** In market-maker models where time
priority grants fill-probability rents
([Avellaneda and Stoikov 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697680701589908) and descendants),
early quotes earn priority value `~ exp(-kappa * queue_ahead)`. Randomized tie-break removes
that rent: the incentive to post early at the touch falls, and the family predicts an
**order-one** response — less early touch quoting, shallower touch depth, wider effective
spreads, changed cancel/replace intensity — not an in-law invariance.

The fork is exactly the program's recurring irreducible question (adaptive agent versus fixed
stochastic operator, Experiments 144–145 and descendants), now attached to one legal market
action with an assignable laboratory treatment. Both resolutions are informative: H0 validates
statistical-queue simulators for rule response and falsifies strategic extrapolation; H1
validates adaptive simulators and motivates the simulator-prediction asset use.

## 4. Selected treatment and primary contrast

- Arms: continuous CDA under (a) strict FIFO time priority within equal price, (b) uniform
  random priority among the equal-price tie set at each match event. Pro-rata as an optional
  third arm only if the precision floor of the asset plan supports it.
- Primary contrast (from the frozen estimand vector): session-level difference in time-weighted
  spread and touch depth; secondary: cancel/replace intensity and impact-response slopes.
- Everything else in the capability-build plan stays frozen: session-level assignment and SUTVA,
  arm-invariant schema with reason-coded rejections and state hashes, sealed confirmation site,
