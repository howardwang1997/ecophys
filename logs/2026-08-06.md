## Session 5 (20:57) — transient-tail interpretation boundary

### Goal

Determine whether the observed heavy-tail transient should be described as simulation error or market
physics.

### What was done

- Separated four distinct interpretations: numerical bug, evaluation/initialization artifact,
  simulator-internal dynamics, and real-market physics.
- Checked each interpretation against the intervention atlas, dose response, estimator limitations, and
  existing real-event pilot.

### Results / findings

- The strongest current interpretation is a reproducible, model-specific non-equilibrium response of
  EcoMD. Channel specificity, five-asset replication, dose saturation, and relaxation make a trivial
  random/numerical error unlikely.
- It is not established market physics. Coherence is imposed by `state_kick`, reduced friction is only a
  market-depth proxy, no empirical L2 mapping has been validated, and the existing real-event pilot found
  no systematic crash-associated tail thickening.
- Therefore, from the market perspective, the effect remains a candidate mechanism that may turn out to
  be model misspecification. The paper must say "physics of the learned simulator" or "model-internal
  non-equilibrium dynamics," not a discovered universal law of markets.
- W0/W2 are still required to exclude observable/scoring and recovery-estimator artifacts; successful
  completion would strengthen the simulator-physics claim but would not by itself validate market physics.

### Open questions / blockers

- There is no pre-registered event study connecting empirical order-flow coherence and liquidity depth
  to the model's intervention coordinates.
- L2 data and independent-simulator replication are absent from the current workshop evidence.

### Next steps

1. Use model-internal language throughout the STODY manuscript.
2. Treat empirical market validation as a separate falsification gate using L2 event windows, matched
   controls, and pre-specified signatures.

## Session 6 (21:00) — paper value of a model failure

### Goal

Judge whether an unvalidated or potentially erroneous simulator phenomenon merits a standalone paper.

### What was done

- Distinguished lack of empirical validation from demonstrated model error.
- Applied a reviewer-2 threshold separately to the STODY dynamics paper and the Sim2Science evaluation
  paper.

### Results / findings

- A one-off implementation bug or EcoMD-only artifact is not a publishable scientific claim by itself.
  A systematic failure mode can be publishable if it is reproducible, causally diagnosed, generalizes
  across model classes, and is accompanied by a detection/correction protocol or a falsifiable empirical
  signature.
- The Sim2Science paper has the stronger failure-mode route: stationarity contamination can become a
  general evaluation result if W4 demonstrates transfer and specificity.
- STODY is currently defensible as a short workshop study of model-internal stochastic dynamics, but it
  becomes weak as a standalone paper if the effect remains EcoMD-specific, empirically unmatched, and
  causally unlocalized. In that outcome it should be folded into the evaluation paper as a stress-test
  case rather than maintained as a separate manuscript.
- A standalone STODY paper should require at least one stronger gate: independent-model replication,
  causal localization plus correction, formal stochastic-dynamics analysis, or a pre-registered empirical
  signature that survives real data.

### Open questions / blockers

- W5 causal decomposition and independent-model shock replication are absent.
- The real-event pilot is negative for a universal crash-tail claim but is not a matched test of the
  simulator's exact intervention coordinates.

### Next steps

1. Set a manuscript-freeze kill rule: without a general mechanism, correction, theory, or empirical
   correspondence, merge the STODY result into the Sim2Science stress-test narrative.
2. Prioritize W3/W4 because the evaluation-error contribution has the clearest publishable route under
   negative empirical validation.
