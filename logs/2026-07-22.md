### Results / findings

2. **The paper describes the wrong architecture.** Both manuscripts and the hero figure call the
   producing model MACE-like/equivariant message passing, but exp 123/126 use
   `pairwise_kind: stochastic_mlp`. MACE-lite is a retained failure case; SPS is the production pair

3. **The July evidence is absent from the June 24 drafts.** Exp 126 strengthens the coherent-kick vs

   support a dose-dependent recovery-time law: SPX reports 350 steps at all eight doses. The supported

4. **G-D1b remains scientifically unclosed.** All 12 Lévy models trained, but the local Pareto JSON still

   exp 123 sets `log_raw_excess_demand: true`, while the exp 126 Lévy configs omit it and currently label
   post-concave-impact ED as `steady_alphaED`.
5. **The generalized burn-in claim is unvalidated.** G-E (neural-SDE second generator) was left manual
   and has not run. Without it, “standard practice” and cross-generator language must be narrowed to an
   EcoMD case study.
6. **The return self-averaging result passes its pre-registered slope criterion but is not yet a clean
   mechanism proof.** Raw ED Hill is nearly flat in N while raw and EWMA-standardized return Hill rise
   strongly. A `sigma_price=0` plus fixed-SNR/post-impact-term decomposition is needed to rule out the
   changing nonlinear-impact/additive-noise mixture before calling this CLT Gaussianization.
7. **Several paper claims need semantic correction:** differentiability enables gradient calibration
   and gradient-based sensitivity/control optimization, not the existence of a shock interface; ED/|rho|
   are simulated latent-flow proxies, not empirical OFI; `liquidity_drop` is an imposed reduction in
   Langevin friction and needs an explicit market-liquidity mapping assumption; an alpha estimate pinned
   to the Hill floor cannot support the “no finite mean” sentence; five crypto events establish no
   systematic heavy-up, not stationarity of real crash tails.
8. **The drafts are not submission artifacts yet.** Markdown sources are 2,367 and 2,308 words with five
   and four cited figures; both TeX files are explicitly stale, no TeX engine is installed locally, and
   no page-fit/render verification exists. The old 4-page plan is implausible without a substantial cut.
9. **Repository docs and declared quality gates are stale.** README still says Phase 2→3, MACE-lite H20
   pending, and 135 tests; current history reaches exp 126 and 400 tests. `scripts/README.md` likewise
   foregrounds obsolete v1 launch flow. Strict typing and lint gates are not currently met.

## Session 4 (23:07) — fewer-H20 and A100/V100 feasibility audit

### Goal

Determine whether the workshop P0 experiments require 8×H20, and quantify safe single-/few-GPU and
A100/V100 alternatives without changing the experimental sample size or seeds.

### What was done

- Verified that inference is rollout-parallel across ranks and that training launchers use one GPU per
  config; the eight cards provide throughput rather than pooled HBM or model parallelism.
- Rechecked current custom-autograd training logs instead of relying on the obsolete default-autograd
  HBM profile: all 12 exp-126 Lévy runs peak at 14.19 GiB allocated / 14.85 GiB reserved, and the
  selected exp-108 neural-SDE runs have essentially the same peak.
- Recomputed P0 throughput from the measured N=10k, T=8000 H20 rollout time of about 168 seconds and
  the measured training times (62.4 min/Lévy config; 36.5 min/neural-SDE config).
- Added few-card schedules, alternative-GPU gates, heterogeneous-worker rules, and an explicit seed
  warning to `papers/proposal/workshop_revision_plan_2026-07-22.md` and lasting project memory.

### Results / findings

- Eight H20s are not required. With checkpoints and W2 trajectories restored, the recommended booking
  is about 48h on 1×H20, 24–30h on 2×H20, 14–18h on 4×H20, or 12h on 8×H20.
- A100 40/80GB and V100 32GB are HBM-feasible for mandatory P0 inference and retraining. V100 16GB is
  too close to the 14.85 GiB reserved peak for safe production retraining.
- Non-H20 runtime cannot be inferred reliably from headline FLOPS because the simulator is sequential
  fp32 code; one full-rollout and one short-training probe are required.
- Card count must not change the 32-rollout sample size. Moreover, current rank-derived seeding means
  changing NPROC silently changes the seed set unless an explicit manifest/rank-block queue is used.

### Open questions / blockers

- No A100/V100 timing or inference-HBM measurements exist in the repository; they require a probe on
  the actual candidate machine.
- The exact normal versus contingency booking still depends on checkpoint and W2 trajectory recovery.

### Next steps

1. Prefer 1–2×H20 if elapsed time permits; otherwise use A100 40/80GB after the two probes.
2. Implement an explicit 32-seed manifest in the consolidated W1–W4 driver before any card-count change.

## Session 5 (23:23) — A100-40G versus H20 cost model

### Goal

Estimate the monetary compute saving from moving the workshop P0 GPU workload from H20 to A100-40G.

### What was done

- Used a same-provider public 8-GPU bare-metal quote to avoid cross-provider distortion: H20 is
  ¥96/node-hour (¥12/GPU-hour), while A100-40G is ¥28/node-hour (¥3.5/GPU-hour).
- Applied the prices to the measured 34.4 H20 GPU-hours for normal P0 core inference and 59.9 H20
  GPU-hours for the full retrain-plus-W2-rerun contingency.
- Computed a slowdown-sensitive cost model instead of estimating A100 time from headline FLOPS:
  `saving fraction = 1 - slowdown * 3.5 / 12`.

### Results / findings

- A100-40G remains cheaper whenever it is less than 3.43× slower than H20 on this simulator.
- Under a conservative unmeasured 2.0–2.5× slowdown, normal P0 active-GPU cost falls from ¥412.8 to
  ¥240.8–301.0, saving ¥111.8–172.0 (27.1–41.7%).
- Under the same range, the 59.9-H20-GPU-hour contingency falls from ¥718.8 to ¥419.3–524.1, saving
  ¥194.7–299.5 (27.1–41.7%).
- These are active-compute comparisons. Keeping an hourly GPU node alive during CPU scoring would be
  wasteful and would distort the comparison.
- If the existing H20 allocation has zero marginal cash cost, renting A100 does not save cash; it only
  frees H20 opportunity cost. The user's actual internal chargeback/rental quote overrides public rates.

### Open questions / blockers

- The repository has no A100 timing benchmark. One full T=8000 rollout is needed to replace the
  2.0–2.5× planning range with an exact cost.
- The quoted ¥3.5/GPU-hour is an effective rate when all eight cards of that listed bare-metal node are
  utilized; a single-card A100 rental may have a different price.

### Next steps

1. Run one fixed-checkpoint, fixed-seed T=8000 A100 rollout and record wall time plus peak HBM.
2. Recalculate with the user's actual A100 and H20 hourly prices before booking capacity.
