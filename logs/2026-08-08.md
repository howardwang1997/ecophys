## Session 4 — simulator-audit versus real-physics boundary

### Goal

Decide whether the completed simulator-discrepancy result is scientifically worth publishing and how
it should relate to the real-market physics program.

### Results / decision

- The exp127 result is worth a narrow non-archival Sim2Science paper because it is a systematic,
  consequential evaluation failure rather than a coding bug: the startup window creates an apparently
  favorable heavy-tail score, the effect is large and held-out robust, and the paper supplies a frozen
  protocol that prevents the misleading interpretation.
- It is not a market-physics result and does not merit PRL/Nature Physics framing. Evidence is confined
  to one simulator family, analytic sensitivity is weak, one primary transfer fails, and E3 coverage is
  incomplete. The E-B label is binding.
- No further experiment should be spent trying to broaden this workshop claim. Submit the completed
  non-archival audit because most cost is already sunk and it improves the credibility of later work,
  then return compute/data effort to phenomena established directly in real markets.
- STODY should run only if it tests a pre-specified empirical signature or a mechanism anchored by real
  data. A second simulator-only transient paper would over-invest in model-internal behavior and should
  be cancelled or merged.

### Next steps

1. Complete user-owned Sim2Science upload actions without expanding the claim.
2. Resume the real-data physics program with model-independent empirical gates and null/surrogate tests.
3. Apply the simulator only after the empirical phenomenon is established, as a controlled mechanism
   probe rather than evidence that the phenomenon exists in markets.
